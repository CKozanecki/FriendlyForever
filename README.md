# Friendly Forever

A courtesy addon for **World of Warcraft: Forever** (TOC interface `16001`).

- **Greetings and thanks:** greets guildmates who log in, welcomes new guild and group members, says hello when you join a group, and thanks players outside your group who buff you, in chat messages you can edit (below).
- **Favours:** when a stranger buffs you, it thanks them, and a button offers your buff back, cast with one press ([Favours](#favours)).
- **Favour ledger:** who buffed you, whether you thanked them, and favours you returned, with titles for returning them ([Favour ledger](#favour-ledger)).
- **Flavour:** the default lines speak in your race's voice and change with where you are and the hour ([Flavour](#flavour)).
- **Control:** an ignore list, snooze, profiles, and export or import of your settings ([Use](#use)).

The chat messages:

| Event | Sent to | Placeholders |
|---|---|---|
| Guild member logs in | Guild chat | `{name}`, `{fullname}` |
| A player outside your group buffs you | Say, a whisper to them, or a `/thank` | `{name}`, `{fullname}`, `{buff}` |
| New member joins the guild | Guild chat | `{name}`, `{fullname}` |
| Player joins your group (not in battlegrounds; joins a few seconds apart share one line, which lists them all; in a raid, only the leader welcomes, at most once every 30 seconds) | Party or raid chat | `{name}`, `{fullname}` |
| You join someone else's group | Party or raid chat | none |
| You rejoin a group (same leader) this session | Party or raid chat | none |
| A party member levels up (not in raids, battlegrounds or arenas) | Party chat (instance chat in a dungeon-finder group) | `{name}`, `{fullname}` |
| Your buff from the buff button reaches them, with its line switched on (off by default) | Say | `{name}`, `{fullname}`, `{buff}` |

`{name}` is the first name ("Nikki") and `{fullname}` the whole name ("Nikki Shifts"). In the two guild greetings (login hello and welcome), `{name}` is the whole name too, as many players use their first name as a title. Forever characters always have a surname. The addon identifies players by their full name, so two people called Nikki are never mixed up.

Placeholders work in any case: `{Buff}`, `{BUFF}` and `{buff}` all fill. One the addon doesn't know, such as a typo like `{bfuf}`, stays as typed, so the preview shows it.

Each message type has its own on/off switch, a delay, a "don't repeat to the same player" cooldown (default 3 minutes; for the two hellos it's per group leader and 30 seconds, and for Level Up 60 seconds; guild hellos also skip anyone greeted in the last 30 minutes) and 30 editable messages. On the **Favours** tab the thank-yous have the switch, delay and repeat cooldown, while the line said when your buff lands has its own switch and 30 lines but no delay or cooldown: it's said once for each favour you return. Lines are used in a shuffled rotation: every line comes up once before any repeats, a new round never starts with the line that just ended the last one, and the rotation carries over across reloads and logins.

## Flavour

The default lines are written in a Warcraft fantasy voice, and some words in them change with the moment:

| Placeholder | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `{greeting}` | A greeting in your race's voice: "Well met", "Throm-ka", "Ishnu-alah", "Good to see ye"… |
| `{thanks}` | A thank-you in your race's voice: "Thank ye kindly", "My thanks"… |
| `{blessing}` | A parting wish chosen by your race, faction, where you are and the hour: "Elune light your path.", "Safe travels through Westfall.", "Rest well tonight." In guild login and guild welcome lines it's chosen without your place, since the guildmate is usually somewhere else |
| `{cheer}` | A rallying cry: "For the Horde!", "For Khaz Modan!" |
| `{zone}` | Where you are ("Elwynn Forest"; "these lands" if the game doesn't say). In guild login and guild welcome lines it's where the guildmate is, from the guild roster; when the roster can't say, a line or phrase that needs it isn't used |
| `{timeofday}` | "morning", "afternoon" or "evening", by the game's clock |
| `{myclass}` | Your class ("mage") |
| `{class}` | Their class, when the game says (a guildmate from the roster, or whoever buffed you); otherwise "friend" |

Every Forever race has its own voice: the eight original races, and both Skyborne peoples. The High Order (Alliance) speaks of ley lines and Eldre'Thalas, the Windshapers (Horde) of the winds and the sky spirits. They share one race name in the game, so the addon tells them apart by race ID, or by faction. `/friendly status` shows how the game reports your race and whose voice you're using.

So "{greeting}, {name}! {blessing}" comes out as "Throm-ka, Thrall! Strength and honor." from an orc, or "Ishnu-alah, Thrall! Elune light your path." from a night elf. The same words aren't used again until a few others have been. Use these in your own lines too.

The 0.5 to 0.6 default lines are still there: each tab's **Load casual lines (0.6)** button puts them back, and **Load fantasy lines** loads the new ones (on the **Favours** tab, they load the set of lines shown: **Thank-you lines** or **When my buff lands**). Lines you edited are never replaced by an update.

## Install

With the CurseForge app, search for **Friendly Forever** and install it for WoW Forever.

By hand: unzip `FriendlyForever-<version>.zip` (or copy the `FriendlyForever` folder) into the AddOns folder of your WoW Forever game folder:

```
<your WoW Forever game folder>/Interface/AddOns/
```

To find the game folder, open the Battle.net app, choose WoW Forever, then the gear next to Play and **Show in Explorer** (**Show in Finder** on a Mac). Beta testers' copies were in `World of Warcraft/_classic_beta_/`.

## Use

- **Start here:** Esc → Options → AddOns → Friendly Forever → **Start here** shows your main settings and keys as they are, with a link to every other page and the walkthrough. A new install starts switched off with the walkthrough open: it shows and explains the main settings a step at a time, and finishing or skipping it turns Friendly Forever on (so does `/friendly on`). If you already used the addon, it shows once and changes nothing.
- **Options:** Esc → Options → AddOns → Friendly Forever, or type `/friendly`. The tabs are Guild Login, Guild Join, Party Join, Party Hello / Hello Again, Level Up, **Favours** (thanking players who buff you, offering your buff back, buff requests and passers-by, the buff button, and the lines for the thanks and for when your buff lands) and **Buffs** (who gets each buff, the buff order, the buff options, and buff mode's key, triggers and top-up), plus the **Guild** page, the **Start here** page, the **General** page (snooze, chat limit, ignore list, profiles, export and import, bug report), the **Favour Ledger** page and the **Weapon buffs** page (experimental).
- **Guild page:** Esc → Options → AddOns → Friendly Forever → **Guild** shows every guildmate with the addon, read-only, who in the guild has it (their version and when they were last seen), who leads, the guild's limits and the vetoed lines. The guild master, and the ranks the guild master picks (the page asks the first time), lead: leaders set the limits (how many copies greet a login or welcome a new member, and guild hellos on or off for the whole guild), see the guild lines each copy said recently, and veto a line so no copy says it again, or undo a veto. Only the guild master picks which ranks lead. The Guild Login and Guild Join tabs say when your guild's limits apply, and a vetoed line's hover says so. Guild settings can only make your copy quieter: your own lower setting stays, and nothing a guildmate sends can make your copy say new words or cast.
- `/friendly find <word>`: which settings match the word, and the page each one is on (`/friendly find snooze`). Where the game's own Settings search can look into addons, it finds them too, through the **Find a setting** page.
- **Minimap button:** left-click opens options, right-click turns the addon on or off, shift-click (either button) turns buff mode on or off (the tooltip shows whether it's on), drag to move it. Friendly Forever is also in the addon compartment (the addon drop-down by the minimap) where the game has one, and its clicks do the same.
- **Key binding:** the buff button's key binding in Key Bindings → Friendly Forever presses the buff button.
- `/friendly on | off | toggle`: turn everything on or off. `/friendly help` lists every command (`/ffr` also works).
- `/friendly test`: preview one message of each type (only you see it). `/friendly say`: send a waiting buff thank-you now. `/friendly minimap`: show or hide the minimap button.
- `/friendly snooze [minutes] | off`: send nothing for a while (15 minutes by default, up to a day). It holds across a reload.
- `/friendly ignore [name]` and `/friendly unignore <name>`: never greet, welcome or thank a player. Use the full name ("Nikki Shifts"); with no name it takes your target, or lists the names when you have no target.
- `/friendly ledger [count] | clear`: the favour ledger (see below).
- `/friendly profile [new | use | delete] <name>`: profiles. Each character switches to the profile it chose when it logs in. The buff settings each character keeps for itself (weapon buffs, buff mode's key, "Offer my self-buffs", the buff order with "Never offer", and who gets each buff) are its own on the Default profile; a character on any other profile, a shared one, uses that profile's. A character that chose Default goes back to it at login. A new character that logs in while another profile is active gets a profile of its own, named after it ("Ann", or "Ann 2" if that name is taken), copied from the settings you had: it never joins the other profile, and changing it changes no other character. With the most profiles allowed (20), it uses the active one instead, and chat says so (#519). Weapon buffs were reset once, for every character, in the update that made them each character's own (#508).
- `/friendly export` and `/friendly import [undo]`: your settings as one line of text, to keep or share. Importing never turns the addon on or off and only adds to your ignore list; `import undo` puts your settings back.
- The **General** page has buttons for snooze, the chat limit, the ignore list, profiles, export and import, and the bug report; the **Favour Ledger** page shows the ledger; the **Weapon buffs** page holds the weapon buff settings.
- The buff button: `/friendly buffback` (what the button holds), `/friendly move` (drag it), `/friendly buffmode` (lend it a key) and `/friendly blessings` (paladins: the Blessings you give in this raid), and `/friendly raid` (the raid leader's buff assignments, experimental). See [Buffing back](#buffing-back).
- Checking what the addon does: `/friendly status`, `/friendly selftest`, `/friendly names`, `/friendly nameplates`, `/friendly debug` and `/friendly report`. See [Troubleshooting](#troubleshooting).
- **Don't send messages while I'm AFK** (on by default, at the top of the options): a greeting or thank-you that comes due while you're AFK is dropped, not saved for later. `/friendly test` and `/friendly selftest` still work.
- **Lines per minute in each chat** (General page, 4 by default, 1 to 10), so the addon stays a helper, not a spammer: the most lines it sends to /say, whispers, emotes, guild chat or group chat in any minute. A thank-you over the limit is dropped, and the ledger says so ("chat rate limit reached"); the first one each session is also mentioned in your chat window, where only you see it; greetings and welcomes wait for room, up to their usual 2 minutes. `/friendly status` counts what the limit held back and dropped.

## Troubleshooting

- `/friendly report` (or **Copy for a bug report** on the General page) shows the status and the debug log as text to copy into a bug report.
- `/friendly status` shows whether the addon is active, the login grace period, guild roster size, chat lockdown, what it has seen and sent, send errors, calls the game blocked, and Lua errors.
- `/friendly selftest` whispers you through the real send path, to prove sending works.
- `/friendly nameplates` turns on friendly player names, which the game needs to name casters.
- `/friendly names` shows exactly how the game reports your name, your target's and the guild roster's, and the name the addon uses.
- `/friendly debug` logs every decision with its reason, both to chat and to `WTF/Account/<account>/SavedVariables/FriendlyForever.lua`. That file is written on `/reload` or logout. Turn debug off again with the same command.
- `/console scriptErrors 1` makes WoW show Lua errors. They are hidden by default.

## Favours

The **Favours** tab in the options holds everything about a player outside your group who buffs you: thanking them, offering your buff back, and the buff button. Buff mode's key, triggers and top-up are on the **Buffs** tab. Top to bottom:

- **When someone buffs you:** Thank them, Delay, **Don't thank the same player again for**, **Name the buffs in the message**, **Tell players I buff that it was me**, **Thank them with**, **If that can't reach them** and **Stay quiet in capital cities**.
- **Return the favour:** **Offer my buff back**, with **Offer for** (minutes) beside it, then **Say a line when my buff lands** (off by default).
- **Asked and passing by:** **Offer my buffs to players who ask in chat** and **Also offer my buffs to passers-by in buff mode**, with its help line and the friendly nameplates warning.
- **The buff button:** **Show the button in capital cities**, **Renew my buff when theirs has under**, and a link to the **Buffs** tab for who gets which buff, the buff order, **Never offer** and buff mode.
- **Lines:** two sub-tabs, **Thank-you lines** and **When my buff lands**, with 30 editable lines each, **Preview random**, **Reset this tab to defaults** (on **Favours** it resets both sets of settings and lines) and **Load fantasy lines** and **Load casual lines** (they load only the sub-tab shown).

Before 1.5 these settings were spread over two tabs. Nothing moved or reset: your settings, edited lines, keys, profiles and settings lines load unchanged, and `/friendly find` finds every moved setting on the **Favours** tab, including by the old tab names. After an update, the walkthrough's **Favours: thanking and buffing back** step shows where each thing moved, including that buff mode's key, triggers and top-up are now on the **Buffs** tab under **Buff mode**. The **Buffs** tab's **Strangers** place is for players outside your group who buff you; passers-by use its rules too.

### Thanking them

No combat log is used. New buffs come from `UNIT_AURA`, and the caster is identified, in order, by:

1. The aura's caster unit (target, mouseover, focus, nameplate, and so on).
2. A matching spell cast seen from one of those units just before the buff landed.
3. A **buff receipt** from the caster's own copy of Friendly Forever (see below).
4. Otherwise the caster is **unidentified**.

**Turn on friendly player nameplates.** The game can only name a caster it has a unit for. Without friendly player nameplates, most strangers who buff you can't be named. The **Favours** tab warns you when they're off, and its **"Show friendly player names"** button (or `/friendly nameplates`) turns them on as names only. The addon never changes this setting by itself.

**Thank them with** (on the **Favours** tab): a line in /say (the default), a whisper, a `/thank` emote, or a line in /say and an emote. The game only allows addons to use `/say` outdoors right after a key press, so /say thanks wait and go out on your next key press or press of the buff button (on any key, mouse button or the mouse wheel), or when you type `/friendly say`. A whisper goes out at once. While the interface is hidden (Alt+Z), key presses don't send waiting say thanks, except a press of the buff button (its key, or the mouse wheel lent to it), which still does. Show the interface again and they go out on your next key press, unless they've waited longer than 2 minutes. The emote is aimed at the caster while the game still has them on a nameplate or as your target, and is a plain `/thank` otherwise. The game doesn't require a key press for emotes, so a `/thank` goes out at once, and one person who buffs you more than once gets a single `/thank` (the repeat cooldown). /say still waits for a key press.

**If that can't reach them:** what happens when the caster is unidentified, or the game can't deliver the whisper ("No player named … is currently playing"):
- **say it in /say as "friend"** (the default; the name is yours to choose),
- **/thank instead**: a `/thank` aimed at them if the game still shows them, otherwise a plain `/thank` ("You thank everyone around you"), or
- **do nothing**. The ledger notes "no fallback chosen".

A fallback is a thank-you too: it goes under the chat limit, and is dropped over it.

While you're in a group, an unidentified caster isn't thanked at all, because party-wide buffs such as a groupmate's totems, auras or shouts can arrive with no caster, and must never be mistaken for a stranger's. Strangers the game can name are still thanked while you're grouped.

**Buff receipts.** When your buff lands on a player outside your group, your copy of the addon tells theirs with one hidden addon whisper, which players never see: "that Power Word: Fortitude was mine". Their copy uses it only when their game can't say who cast a buff on them, so it can thank you by name instead of "friend" and offer your buff back. A buff whose caster the game can't name waits 2 seconds for a receipt before it's thanked as "friend". A receipt only names the caster of a buff that really arrived, of the same kind, within a few seconds; on its own it never causes a cast, a thank-you or an offer. Receipts from someone on your ignore list or in your group are ignored, and two players claiming one buff name nobody. None are sent to your group, inside instances, during chat lockdown, or while **Thank them** or **Tell players I buff that it was me** (on by default, on the **Favours** tab) is off.

Buffs from one person that arrive before the thank-you goes out are combined: *"Thank you for the Power Word: Fortitude and Divine Spirit, Bob!"*. The repeat cooldown then stops any second thank-you.

**Buff exchanges.** When you trade buffs with someone, your thank-you waits until the exchange is done, so it comes after your buff back and lists everything they gave you. It waits at least 2 seconds after the last buff either way, and while the buff button holds them: up to 5 seconds, or, if they run the addon too, until you cast it or let it go. Their copy and yours tell each other what your buttons hold, so each also waits for the other's buff. A thank-you always goes within 30 seconds of the first buff it thanks for, and their later buffs in the same exchange aren't thanked again. An exchange ends 10 seconds after its last buff.

**Quiet in capital cities** (on by default). In Stormwind, Ironforge, Darnassus, Orgrimmar, Thunder Bluff and Undercity (and Valanaar and Dalaran, once their map numbers are confirmed in game: [#207](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/207)), buffs come in waves from people running past, so no thank-yous go out there. The favour ledger still counts every buff. The buff button stays hidden there too, unless you tick **"Show the button in capital cities"** on the **Favours** tab. Favours are still collected, and show once you leave if they haven't run out. Turn buff mode on in a city and thanks and the button work as usual. A stranger's buff doesn't turn buff mode on by itself in a city, since that would end the quiet. Inns, small towns and other rested areas aren't capitals. Greetings aren't affected. `/friendly status` shows which city the addon thinks you're in. The six capitals' map numbers were checked in game at launch; Valanaar's and Dalaran's are still to be checked (#207).

### Buffing back

When someone outside your group buffs you, a small button appears with their name and the buff you can give back. Press it, or the key you set for it in **Key Bindings → Friendly Forever** (the buff button's key binding, with or without Shift/Ctrl/Alt), and it casts your buff on them and gives you your own target back. The game only lets an addon cast through a button you press, so nothing is ever cast without your key press or click.

- **Which buff:** your class's buffs that you know, in your order. On the **Buffs** tab you can move each buff up or down and mark any of them **Never offer**; the order and marks are this character's own (or its shared profile's, #508) and go with export, and each class keeps its own. It skips any they already wear (when the game lets the addon see), mana buffs for classes without mana, buffs your **Strangers** rule on the **Buffs** tab leaves them out of (below), and buffs that only reach your own party. A druid gives Mark of the Wild, or Thorns to someone who already has the Mark. A buff of yours they wear that is partly worn off counts as missing, so you renew it: set when with **Renew my buff when theirs has under N% left** on the **Favours** tab (0 to 90, default 70; 0 waits until it's gone). It never renews over a stronger rank than yours or a group version such as Gift of the Wild (your single buff would leave them less time), nor when the game won't say how long their buff has left.
- **Which rank:** every buff for another player (a favour, a request, your group, a passer-by) is cast at the highest rank you know that their level allows: a rank reaches players up to 10 levels below the level it's learned at, as the game requires, so a low-level player gets a lower rank instead of "Target is too low level". Only a lower rank is named in the press (`Mark of the Wild(Rank 5)`); your highest is cast by its name, as before. A player too low for every rank of a buff is offered your next buff; one whose level the game won't show is skipped, and the button says why. Rank levels come from Forever's own spell data ([#459](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/459)); the game's side is still to be confirmed in game ([#463](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/463)). When the game refuses a cast for a reason about the player (out of range, out of sight, too low level, not a valid target), the button leaves them for 15 seconds and offers the next one; a refusal about you holds nobody (see **Feedback** below).
- **Water breathing:** a warlock's Unending Breath and a shaman's Water Breathing are offered only near water: while you're swimming or underwater, for 2 minutes after you leave the water, or in an underwater dungeon (Blackfathom Deeps, The Drowned City). This goes for buff mode too. The game can't tell the addon whether someone else is swimming, so it goes by you. Switch **Water breathing: only near water** off on the **Buffs** tab to offer them anywhere.
- **Requests in chat** (off by default): tick **Offer my buffs to players who ask in chat** on the **Favours** tab, and a player who whispers you, or says in /say or party chat (not raid, guild or yell), a short request for one of your buffs is put on the button with "asked for" and that buff. Examples: "mark pls", "int please", "can I get fort?", "any kings?". It never casts by itself, and nothing is said in chat. The message must be just the request: an optional "can I get", "could I have", "any", "need" or "got", then the buff's name or a common nickname, then an optional "pls", "please", "plz", "ty" or "?". "I'll mark the boss" queues nobody, and neither does a request that names no buff ("buff pls"). The names:
  - druid: mark, motw, mark of the wild, gotw, gift, gift of the wild; thorns;
  - mage: int, intellect, ai, arcane intellect, ab, brilliance, arcane brilliance;
  - paladin: kings, bok; wisdom, wis, bow; might, bom (each also as "blessing of ...");
  - priest: fort, fortitude, pwf, stam, stamina, power word fortitude; spirit, ds, divine spirit; shadow prot, shadow protection, sprot;
  - shaman and warlock: water breathing, wb, unending breath, ub, breath (either buff, by either name).
  - shaman: water walking, ww, walk on water. Water Walking is offered only when asked for: a favour and buff mode never offer it unasked, and it isn't held back away from water. The **Buffs** tab lists it as "(when asked)". Like any request, it's offered even when it's marked **Never offer**.

  The spell's own name counts too. Any buff of yours you know counts, even one marked **Never offer** (a request is always offered while this setting is on), but never one their class can't use (Intellect to a warrior), and never Battle Shout, which only reaches your own party. Requests from members of your own group count too. They're offered by the same rules as a favour (they must be in sight and reach, and the PvP rule applies), but only with the buff they asked for, and not if they already wear it. A water buff they ask for is offered away from water too. One request per player waits at a time, and asking again doesn't move them up. At most 5 wait at once, the oldest going first, and a request never pushes out a favour. They run out after the favours' time. Someone you already owe gets the buff they asked for when you return their favour. Requests aren't taken from players on your ignore list, while snoozed, in a battleground once the battle starts, or in a capital city while the button is hidden there. Turning the setting off lets waiting requests go. A press says "Gave Thorns to Bob Stone, who asked for it." No line for a landed buff is said for a request.
- **Who:** the newest favour first. It waits while they're dead. It never offers a buff to someone the game shows flagged for PvP unless you're flagged too, since buffing them would flag you. The one exception is a member of your group inside a dungeon or raid: once every member of your group is in there with you, a favour owed to a flagged member is offered like any other, since nobody can attack you there (the same rule as [buff mode](#buff-mode)). While anyone is still outside, offline or can't be read, and everywhere else (the open world, cities, battlegrounds, arenas), it isn't; nor, anywhere, is a favour owed to a flagged player outside your group. Their flag, and the rule, are read again at the moment you press. A favour is offered only while the game shows them (on a nameplate, as your target or under your mouse) and your buff can reach them. If they've left, out of sight or out of range, the button moves on to the next person you owe, or hides if there's no one else; they're offered again if they come back before the favour runs out. Friendly nameplates help it see them. When the button can't fit its reason on one line, it grows to show all of it. Favours are dropped after 10 minutes (adjustable on the **Favours** tab), and are forgotten on a reload or logout.
- **No endless trading:** one round each way, at most. Every buff of yours the game says reached another player is remembered, whether the button cast it or you did by hand, and their buff back within 10 minutes answers it rather than being a new favour. A buff you cast by hand on someone you owe returns their favour too. When you return a favour, your copy tells theirs (with a hidden addon message) that your buff answers theirs, so they don't owe you one back, however long the favour waited. If the game names your target by first name only, the addon remembers the buff only when exactly one player of that name is your target, mouseover, focus or soft target, and neither you nor anyone in your group shares it, so a buff from someone else with that first name is never mistaken for an answer.
- **Right-click** the button to let a favour go.
- **Feedback:** what each press did, under the button (and in chat where it needs your attention):
  - returned: "Returned Mark of the Wild to Bob Stone.";
  - the game doesn't say who it reached: "Cast Mark of the Wild, but the game didn't say it reached Bob Stone." The favour isn't counted as returned;
  - it went to someone else: "Mark of the Wild went to Ann Vale, not Bob Stone." in chat, a few seconds after the press, and the favour stays. The buff counts as a buff to Ann, as a cast by hand does. A cast by hand on someone else while the button waits is treated the same way, but if the button's own cast follows, the button reports that instead;
  - the game refuses it: its reason in chat ("Couldn't buff Bob Stone: Out of range."), or "Couldn't buff Bob Stone (the game gave no reason).", and the favour stays. A refusal about them (out of range, out of sight, too low level, not a valid target) has the next press try someone else for 15 seconds; one about you ("Not enough mana", in combat, a form) holds nobody: the button stays on them, and with the key held down chat says it at most once every few seconds. A press in the global cooldown ("Spell is not ready yet") isn't a refusal: nothing is said, and the next press offers the same player. The same goes for your group, passers-by and your self-buffs;
  - nothing was cast: "No answer: couldn't find Bob Stone (out of sight?)." in chat;
  - the server never answers (a lag spike): "No word back on Mark of the Wild for Bob Stone. If it didn't land, press again.";
  - the favour had already gone (it expired, or you let it go) by the time the cast landed: "Cast Mark of the Wild on Bob Stone (no favour was waiting).";
  - right-click: "Let Bob Stone go."

  A press's line goes as soon as the button offers a different player, so it never sits under someone else's offer ([#461](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/461)); `/friendly buffback` still shows the last one. A right-click's line stays its few seconds wherever the button moves.

  Pressing again while a cast is still on its way is fine: each answer is matched to its own press where the game says which, and otherwise to the newest.
- **After a /reload in a fight** the button appears when the fight ends.
- **Say a line when my buff lands** (off by default, on the **Favours** tab; it stays on for players who had it on before 1.5): 30 editable lines, with the flavour placeholders and `{buff}`. The line is said only after the game confirms your buff reached them, never for a cast that failed, went to someone else or found nobody. It goes the way **Thank them with** says: a line in /say (also for a line and an emote), a whisper to them, or a plain `/thank` aimed at them for the emote. Like a thank-you, a /say line goes out on your next key press or buff button press (the game only lets /say follow one) and is dropped if that takes more than 30 seconds; a `/thank` needs no key press.
- **In combat** the button is empty: it clears itself as a fight starts and fills again afterwards. The game locks secure buttons in fights, so whatever it held would otherwise stay on it, aimed at the world outside the fight.
- **Say a line** only while the addon may speak: not while snoozed, or AFK with "Don't send messages while I'm AFK" on.
- `/friendly move` unlocks the button so you can drag it, and locks it again. `/friendly buffback` shows exactly what the button holds and why, for checking in game.

### Buff mode

Buff mode lends the button a key of your choice while it's on, the mouse wheel by default, so you can buff quickly by scrolling. When buff mode ends, the key does what it did before. The key is lent only while buff mode is on, not bound, so Key Bindings shows it as Not Bound. It never casts by itself: each cast still needs your press, the key only decides which press.

- **On and off:** shift-click the minimap button (its tooltip shows whether buff mode is on), or `/friendly buffmode` switches it, `/friendly buffmode on` or `off` sets it, and **Key Bindings → Friendly Forever → "Friendly Forever: buff mode on/off"** gives it a key. It needs the addon and **Offer my buff back** on, and it's off after a login or reload.
- **The key:** on the **Buffs** tab, under **Buff mode**, click **Buff mode key** and press a key, scroll the wheel or click a side mouse button (Esc cancels), with Shift, Ctrl or Alt if you like. Or type `/friendly buffmode key MOUSEWHEELDOWN`. Esc, Enter, `/`, and the left and right mouse buttons can't be lent, nor the key that switches buff mode. A fight starting cancels the wait for a key. A key that can't be used, or that the game won't lend, falls back to Mouse Wheel Up, with a message.
- **Your group:** in buff mode the button also offers your buffs to party or raid members missing them, one per press, after any favours you owe. Your first buff goes to everyone missing it before your second goes to anyone, you included (you come first for each buff, and it casts on you without changing your target), in your buff order, skipping buffs their class can't use and your self-buffs (below). It uses the same safety rules as a favour: it skips anyone dead, offline, out of your spell's range or too far away for the game to show, and anyone flagged for PvP unless you're flagged too (read as you press). The one exception is inside a dungeon or raid: once every member of your group is in there with you, a member flagged for PvP gets your buffs like anyone else (single buffs, group versions and Greater Blessings), since nobody can attack you there. While anyone is still outside, offline or can't be read, and everywhere else (the open world, cities, battlegrounds, arenas), a flagged member is never buffed unless you're flagged too. A member whose name or buffs can't be read is skipped, and the button says why. So is one whose flag can't be read, except where flagged members may be buffed (you're flagged too, or that exception): nobody's flag is asked for there. The game can't tell an addon about line of sight, so if it refuses a cast for a reason about them (out of sight, say), that member waits 15 seconds and the next press goes to the next member; a refusal about you (no mana) holds nobody. A paladin gives each member one Blessing (in your buff order) and never casts another over it, since that would replace it. Group buffs never say a line and aren't thanked or counted as favours. "Gave Mark of the Wild to Ann Vale." shows under the button; right-click skips that member for 5 minutes, and Shift+right-click puts them on that buff's **Never** list (below).
- **Top-up timing:** a buff you give (whoever cast it) on a group member with less than 5 minutes left counts as missing, so you can renew it before it runs out. Set the minutes with **Top up my buffs with under** on the **Buffs** tab, under **Buff mode** (0 to 15; 0 waits until it's gone). A buff that lasts 10 minutes or less, such as Thorns, counts only with under 1 minute left, whatever the setting (unless it's 0), so it isn't offered half-way through. It renews only a rank you can cast over (not a higher rank than yours or a scroll; a group version, such as Prayer of Fortitude or a Greater Blessing, counts as the single rank of the same strength, and the group version renews it only when it is at least as strong), never a buff no longer than the time it's renewed at (the setting, or 1 minute for a short buff), and never when the game won't say how long a buff has left. Strangers are never topped up.
- **Solo:** when you're not in a group, buff mode still offers you your own buffs (Fortitude, Arcane Intellect, Mark of the Wild, a Blessing...) when you're missing them or they're running low, one per press, cast on you without changing your target, after any favours you owe and before your self-buffs (below). The same rules as in a group: your buff order, **Never offer**, who gets each buff (the **Party** rules: a buff for Tanks, such as Thorns by default, needs the game to say your role is tank; your own name on a buff's **Always** list gets it anyway) and the **Top up** time. Single buffs only, never a group version. Someone else's copy on you counts until it's running low. Nothing is offered while you're dead, or when your buffs, or whether you're in a group, can't be read. Solo, one of your own buffs missing or running low turns buff mode on by itself, by the same rules as in a group (below); buff mode that turned itself on waits for your own buffs before ending. **Offer my own buffs when I'm not in a group** (the **Buffs** tab's **Party** place, on by default) switches it off.
- **Your self-buffs:** once nobody is owed a favour and your group has your buffs (you included), buff mode offers the self-buffs you're missing, one per press, cast on you without changing your target: a priest's Inner Fire, a mage's Ice (or Frost) Armor or Mage Armor, a warlock's Demon Armor (or Demon Skin), a shaman's Lightning Shield, a warrior's Battle Shout, a hunter's Aspect and Trueshot Aura, a paladin's aura and Righteous Fury. Solo too. They're listed on the **Buffs** tab with your other buffs, marked "(you only)", in your buff order with **Never offer** (Righteous Fury starts as Never offer: untick it if you tank); **Offer my self-buffs in buff mode** (on by default) switches them off. One of a kind at a time: wearing any armor, Aspect or aura of yours counts, and when you wear none the first in your order is offered. An Aspect or an aura stays on until you change it, so it's never renewed and never replaces the one you chose (Aspect of the Cheetah included); it's cast with `!` so a second press can't switch it off. Another paladin's aura or another hunter's Aspect of the Wild isn't yours, but another warrior's Battle Shout or another hunter's Trueshot Aura counts, and yours is never cast over it. Omen of Clarity is a passive talent on Forever, so it isn't offered. Trueshot Aura lasts 30 minutes on Forever, and all five ranks are recognised (#379). Others are renewed when running low: with 1 minute left if they last 10 minutes or less (Inner Fire, Lightning Shield), else under your **Top up** time; Top up 0 never renews (charges aren't counted). Nothing is offered while you're dead, mounted, on a flight path, a druid in a form or a shaman in Ghost Wolf, or when the game says the spell can't be cast now (not enough rage or mana, Shadowform); the button says why. Nothing is offered either when your own buffs can't be read. Right-click skips your self-buffs for 5 minutes. In a group, a missing self-buff never turns buff mode on; solo it does, while this option is on. Buff mode that turned itself on waits for them before "everyone has your buffs" ends it. Self-buffs are never favours, never thanked and never said in chat. Their spell IDs come from the game's own spell data ([#44](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/44)); the `!` and the usable check are still to be confirmed in game ([#326](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/326)).
- **Your weapon buffs (experimental, off by default):** on the **Weapon buffs** page, tick **Offer weapon buffs in buff mode** and choose a kind for each hand: a sharpening stone, a weightstone, a wizard oil or a mana oil, a rogue's poison (Instant, Deadly, Crippling, Mind-numbing or Wound), or for the main hand a shaman's Rockbiter, Flametongue, Frostbrand or Windfury Weapon. Each list shows only what your class can use. Once your favours, your group and your self-buffs are done, buff mode offers it for a hand with no stone, oil, poison or imbue on it: the best rank you carry and can use, main hand first. One press puts it on (an item is used, then put on that hand); nothing ever goes on by itself. A hand that already has one is left alone, so nothing is ever replaced early. Nothing is offered for a hand with no weapon (a shield or a held item), or whose weapon has its own Use effect (apply it by hand: the press could fire that effect instead; the same goes for an imbue), in combat, while you're resting (in a city, an inn or a town with one: weapon buffs cost money, so they wait until you leave), while you're dead, mounted or on a flight path, or when the game won't say what's on your weapons or in your bags; the button says why. A druid in a form or a shaman in Ghost Wolf is offered them too: a press may drop you out of the form to put it on, just as using it by hand would (the addon never cancels a form itself), and if the game refuses instead, the button shows why. Right-click skips your weapon buffs for 5 minutes. Every ID comes from Forever's own data ([#45](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/45)); the press itself is still to be confirmed in game ([#409](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/409)).
- **Who gets each buff** (the **Buffs** tab): each place has its own rule for each buff. **Strangers** is for players outside your group who buff you (passers-by use its rules too); **Party**, **Raid** and **PvP** (battlegrounds and arenas) are for buff mode in a party (and solo), a raid group, and a battleground before its gates open or an arena. A buff's **Who gets it** button opens its list: tick **Everyone**, a role (**Tanks**, **Healers**, **Damage**; not for strangers, whose role can't be seen), classes, or press **Off**. Any tick covers a member. Tanks are the group role Tank or a raid's Main Tank; healers and damage are the roles players chose. A member with no role chosen gets a buff only from Everyone, a class or a name, and one whose role can't be read gets no buff by role; the button says why. **Names** opens a buff's **Always** and **Never** lists for that place, separated by commas; Never wins, and Always names get it even when it's Off. Where the game won't say which place you're in, a buff goes only where every place it could be gives it. The defaults: everyone, except Thorns to tanks, and Wisdom and Might to the classes that use them; in PvP every buff is off. A player who asks for a buff in chat gets it whatever these say. After the update to this version, the tab first asks how to start: **Keep my settings** (your rules go to Party and Raid; PvP starts off) or **Use the new defaults** (recommended if you never changed them); until you choose, everything works as before. The rules are this character's own (or its shared profile's, #508) and go with settings lines.
- **My Blessings in this raid** (paladins): when another paladin covers Kings, say, choose the Blessings you give. In a raid, tick them under **Blessings I give in this raid** on the **Buffs** tab's **Raid** place, or type `/friendly blessings might wisdom` (`/friendly blessings all` stops choosing: all of them again, or your share of the raid buff split, below, when there is one; `/friendly blessings` alone says what you give). Buff mode then passes over the others, and a Blessing of yours that you've left to others no longer stops the one you now give. The choice is never saved: it's forgotten as soon as you're not in a raid, or you're in another one (the raid's leader changed, or most of the members it had when you chose have gone), so every raid starts with all your Blessings (or the split's share). In a battleground or an arena it isn't used, and can't be changed there except to stop it; it applies again when you're back in your raid.
- **Raid buff split:** in a raid, copies of Friendly Forever share out the buffs their class has in common, so players of a class don't all give the same one. Three priests give Fortitude, Spirit and Shadow Protection between them; of two paladins, one gives Kings and the other Wisdom and Might. Buff mode then offers you only your share (group versions included), still under your **Raid** rules for who gets each buff; the button's tooltip says what your share is and who has the rest, and `/friendly status` shows the split. Players of a class who outnumber its buffs split the raid's groups (two mages: groups 1-4 and 5-8); a spare paladin waits as a backup. The split follows each player's buff order, then their names, so every copy works out the same one without a leader. **Blessings I give in this raid** shows a paladin's share; setting it by hand still works, and the others share out the rest around it. You take part while buff mode is on and for 5 minutes after; a player who leaves, goes offline or isn't heard from for 3 minutes has their share picked up by the others. **Share buffs with other copies in raids** (the **Buffs** tab's **Raid** place, on by default) switches it off. Players without the addon are never given a share, and with no other copy of your class in the raid, or outside a raid, everything is offered as before. Copies tell each other only which of their class's buffs they give; nothing they say can cast, send chat or change your settings.
- **Raid leader's assignments** (experimental: tried in tests only so far; tell us how it goes): the raid leader and raid assistants can set who gives which buff to which groups, in `/friendly raid` (for example Fortitude: "Ann Vale 1-4, Bob Lake 5-8"; Kings: "Cid Rock"). Copies in the raid then offer an assigned buff only to the groups it's assigned for, over the raid buff split and over Blessings set by hand; buffs nobody is assigned stay with the split. An assignment to someone offline, out of the raid, without the addon, or not offering that buff falls back to the split for that buff. They're shared over the hidden channel and kept for this session: leaving the raid forgets them, and a new raid leader clears them. Each copy checks the sender's rank in its own raid roster on every message; an assignment can only narrow what your copy offers, never cast or change your settings. Members see the window read-only.
- **Passers-by** (off by default: **Also offer my buffs to passers-by in buff mode**, on the **Favours** tab under **Asked and passing by**): in buff mode, once your favours, requests, your group, your self-buffs and your weapon buffs are done, the button also offers your buffs to players nearby who aren't in your group, one press each: a buff they're missing, or one with less than half its time left, as your **Strangers** rules on the **Buffs** tab allow. Only in the open world, never in a dungeon, raid, battleground, arena or capital city, never in a fight; players in a fight come last. Never anyone flagged for PvP while you aren't, the dead, the other faction, or anyone whose details the game won't show. One Blessing of yours each. Nothing is said to them. Right-click skips one for 10 minutes; a buff that reaches them isn't offered to them again for 10 minutes. It needs friendly nameplates.
- **On and off by itself** (**Buffs** tab, under **Buff mode**): buff mode can turn on when you're in a dungeon or raid with your group, when you join a group with another member close by (within about 40 yards, checked for 2 minutes after joining), when someone in your group dies after the group's first pull, when someone outside your group buffs you, when a campfire's warmth, its smoke nearby or a camp benefit is added to you (**at a campfire**), or, out of combat in any group or solo, when a member close by (you included; solo, you alone, your self-buffs too) is missing a buff of yours or has it running low: under 1 minute left for a buff of 10 minutes or less (Thorns), under your **Top up** time for a longer one, the same rule the button tops up by. If you turn buff mode off yourself, the buffs that were low or gone then don't turn it back on until they've been cast again; another buff, or another member's, running low or fading does. These are on by default; the first time each session buff mode turns itself on, chat says where to switch them off. It turns off again at the first pull, once everyone has your buffs (never assumed when someone's buffs can't be read), or after 5 minutes (adjustable; 0 for never), whichever comes first. Turned on in a dungeon or raid, it stays on until every member is in there with you and has your buffs (someone offline, out of sight or unreadable isn't in), or until the first pull, whichever comes first; the 5 minutes start at the first pull or once you leave. **What it gives:** in a group, buff mode offers your buff over the same buff from anyone outside the group (or whose caster the game doesn't name). A buff another group member put up is theirs until it's running low, when top-up renews it; a group version (Arcane Brilliance and the like) isn't offered while it would replace one, and that party gets single buffs. On entering a dungeon or raid, whoever buffs first: a buff (yours or a member's) put up more than 5 minutes before you entered is given again once, until the first pull, so everyone's timers line up; one put up since then is left alone, and the button says so in red for 2 seconds ("Already done by Ann"), empty, before the next buff you need. It never casts over a stronger rank than you know, keeps one Blessing of yours on each player, follows the raid buff split in a raid, and leaves a buff alone when the game won't say who cast it or when. These only end buff mode that turned itself on: buff mode you turn on yourself stays on until you turn it off. With passers-by on, buff mode that turned itself on also waits while the button offers a passer-by a buff, whatever turned it on ([#462](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/462)); only the campfire turns it on for a passer-by. In a resting area (a town with an inn, or a capital), passers-by never turn it on or keep it on, though the button still offers them while it's on for something else; and after you turn buff mode off yourself, they don't turn it back on until you change zone ([#484](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/484)). Nothing turns on in a fight; a trigger that fires in one waits for it to end. The triggers only switch buff mode; they never cast.
- **Battlegrounds:** before the gates open, buff mode and its triggers work as in any raid. Once the battle starts, the addon goes quiet for the rest of the battleground: buff mode ends and nothing turns it on again (you included), the buff button hides, buffs you get aren't thanked or owed, and no lines are sent; guild hellos still go to guild chat. The battle counts as started when the game doesn't say it hasn't (the Preparation buff on you, or the countdown to the gates). Everything is back to normal when you leave, or when the battleground ends. **Buff in a battleground's preparation phase** (the **Buffs** tab's **PvP** place, on by default) switched off keeps the preparation phase quiet too. Every buff is off in **PvP** unless you turn it on there.
- **The button** shows "Buff mode: Mouse Wheel Up" above it, and stays up while buff mode is on, saying why there's nothing to buff when there isn't.
- **In a druid's form or Ghost Wolf** (but not Moonkin Form, where a druid casts every buff without leaving it) the key doesn't cast your buffs, so scrolling never takes you out of the form: the button still shows the buff and says "click to buff (you're in a form)" or "click to buff (you're in Ghost Wolf)", and a click on it casts (the game takes you out of the form). **Fast-cast buffs in form** on the **Buffs** tab (off by default) lets the key cast them there too. Weapon buffs work with the key either way. When the game won't say whether you're in a form, or which, the key waits too. Turned on in such a form, buff mode's chat line says to click the button rather than naming the key.
- **In a fight** the key is handed back as the fight starts, so it does its own job (the button is empty in fights anyway), and lent again when it ends. The game doesn't let key bindings change during a fight, so turning buff mode on or off in one happens when the fight ends.

The button's macro is `/cleartarget`, `/targetexact First Surname`, `/cast [help,nodead] <buff>`, then `/targetlasttarget` (or `/cleartarget` if you had no target). Targeting the exact name means a player whose name only starts the same is never buffed, and the cast only goes off on a friendly, living target, so if they can't be found nothing is cast and your target comes back. The macro worked in the launch-day test pass (Nov 4). Casting on yourself with `[@player]` was added since, and confirmed in game on 17 Nov ([#213](https://github.com/CKozanecki/FriendlyForever/issues/213)); the macro lives in one function, so adjusting it is a small change.

## Favour ledger

Every buff from someone outside your group, while **Thank them** or **Offer my buff back** is on, goes in the ledger: who it was, which buffs, whether you thanked them and how ("in /say", "by whisper", "with /thank"), or why not, and whether you buffed them back. Returning favours earns a title, from **Courteous** at 10 to **Friend to All Azeroth** at 1,000. The reasons a favour wasn't thanked look like this ("thanked them a moment ago", "snoozed", "you were AFK", "on your ignore list"). It counts today's favours and your all-time totals. See it on the **Favour Ledger** page or with `/friendly ledger`. The newest 200 are kept.

## Safety

- Everything sent is cleaned first. `|` escape codes and control characters are removed, and each message is cut to 255 bytes without splitting characters.
- **Guild hellos** (#38): everyone with the addon greets a guildmate who logs in, but each waits a random 1 to 8 seconds on top of the delay (15 seconds by default, so the hello lands after the guildmate has loaded in), and copies tell each other (with hidden guild addon messages) which line they'll use, so no two say the same one. A member who got a hello isn't greeted again if they log back in within 30 minutes, whatever the repeat cooldown is (a /reload forgets it). At most 5 copies greet one login (**Most players with Friendly Forever who greet one login**, Guild Login tab, 1 to 20; a guild leader's limit on the Guild page can lower it for everyone); the rest stay quiet, and debug mode says why. A copy that can't send addon messages (inside an instance, say) greets after its random wait anyway; one that can't tell how many others greeted (its message never came back, or a crowd of messages was dropped) stays quiet, except that until one of its messages has come back in a session, it greets anyway.
- Output is rate-limited (one message per 1.5 s). At most 25 messages can wait, and none waits longer than 2 minutes, so a wave of guild logins can't flood chat.
- On top of that, each chat type gets at most a set number of lines in any minute (4 by default; see [Use](#use)). Everything the addon says goes through one queue that checks the limit, and nothing can get round it. The count survives a reload, and a clock that jumps (the date or time changed, or the computer restarted) can't let a burst through or block sending for more than a minute.
- Nothing is sent during the game's chat lockdown. Messages wait until it ends.
- The 10 seconds after login or reload are ignored, and buffs you already had (re-sent by the game when you log in, reconnect or zone) are never thanked.
- Saved settings are type-checked and clamped on load, so a corrupted or hand-edited file can't break the addon. If they're damaged while you play (by another addon or a bad `/run`), they're repaired as soon as they cause an error in the addon, rather than at the next reload. The ignore list, snooze, profiles and the favour ledger are checked each time they're used, so damage there is repaired before it can cause one.
- The buff button won't offer a buff to a player the game shows flagged for PvP unless you're flagged too (see [Buffing back](#buffing-back)). The one exception is a member of your group, once your whole group is inside a dungeon or raid with you: buff mode buffs them, and the button returns their favour (see [Buffing back](#buffing-back) and [Buff mode](#buff-mode)). It's empty in a fight, and its cast only goes off on a friendly, living target.
- Buff mode only lends a key to the button with an override binding, never changes your saved key bindings, and never lends Esc or Enter. The key is handed back when buff mode ends, during fights, while the addon or **Offer my buff back** is off, and on every login or reload.
- "Secret" values from the Midnight-era API are detected and never used.
- The addon never runs text as code, so other players have no way to control it. Whispers, /say and party chat are read only for [buff requests](#buffing-back), and only while they're switched on (they're off by default). A request is compared, as plain text, with a fixed list of your buffs' names. Anything with an escape code, anything longer than a short request and anything else around the buff's name is ignored. A request can only put the sender on the buff button, which still casts only on your press, and nothing is ever said back.
- **Other copies of Friendly Forever:** copies talk to each other with hidden addon messages, which players never see. **No chat channel is ever joined.** Messages go only over the game's own whisper, party, raid, instance and guild routes, so General, Trade and LocalDefense keep their numbers.
  - Every message from another player is treated as untrusted. It's dropped, and counted in `/friendly status`, if it's unreadable, too long, malformed, from another protocol version, about someone other than its sender, or part of a flood.
  - A message can only tell this copy something, such as "this player runs Friendly Forever". It can never make the addon cast, say anything in chat or change your settings. A buff receipt only puts the sender's name, as the server gives it, on a buff that really arrived and whose caster the game wouldn't name.
  - The addon sends at most a few messages at once and then one every two seconds, well below the game's limit, and drops what it can't send soon.
  - While the addon is switched off, it sends nothing and ignores what it receives. Everything works without other copies, or when the game blocks addon messages (as it may in instances).
- The buff button's macro is built only from a checked player name and the game's own spell name, and each part is refused if it could end its line or start another command (a line break, a `|` escape code, or a leading `/`).
- A pasted settings line (`/friendly import`) is read as data only, with limits on its size and depth, and every value in it is checked like the saved file before it's used.

## Development

Requires Python 3 (to install the Lua tools) and `zip`/`unzip`.

```bash
make setup     # once: Lua 5.1, LuaRocks, busted, luacheck into ~/.cache/friendlyforever
make check     # lint + tests + package
```

- `make lint`: luacheck (Lua 5.1 rules; fails on any undeclared global)
- `make test`: busted suite in `spec/` against a simulated WoW client (`spec/support/wow.lua`)
- `make package`: builds `dist/FriendlyForever-<version>.zip` in the standard addon layout. `.pkgmeta` gives the same result with the CurseForge packager.

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs all three on every pull request and every push to `main`. It also builds the zip with the BigWigs packager that releases use (without uploading) and fails if its files differ from `make package`'s.

### Releasing to CurseForge

Pushing a version tag runs `.github/workflows/release.yml`, which builds with the BigWigs packager, uploads to CurseForge and creates the GitHub release. 0.8.0 was the first file on CurseForge and 1.0.0 is the launch release. [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md#releasing-maintainer) has the full steps; in short:

1. In a pull request, bump `## Version` in `FriendlyForever.toc` and gather the waiting notes in `changes/` into a matching `## <version>` section at the top of `CHANGELOG.md` with `make changelog VERSION=<version>`, as CONTRIBUTING describes (the tests check they agree). That section becomes the CurseForge release notes.
   The CurseForge page description (`docs/curseforge-<major.minor>.md`) changes only when a feature is added or changes, never with patch notes (CONTRIBUTING has the rules).
2. After it merges, tag the merge commit and push only that tag: `git tag vX.Y.Z && git push origin vX.Y.Z` (not `--tags`). Version tags can't be moved or deleted once pushed.
3. The workflow checks that the tag is on `main` and matches the TOC version, then runs lint and tests with no secrets. Only then does the upload job wait for approval in the `release` environment. The CurseForge API token is the secret `CF_API_KEY` in that environment, and the TOC needs `## X-Curse-Project-ID`.
